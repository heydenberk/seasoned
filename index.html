Four dates that matter

Mid-April. Asparagus and ramps arrive. Before this the only things coming out of open ground are roots that overwintered and a handful of bitter greens.

Late July. Tomatoes and corn hit peak together. Five weeks.

The first hard frost, usually early November. Basil is finished overnight. Plenty of other things do the reverse: kale, sprouts, parsnips and carrots convert starch to sugar as a defence against the cold, so a parsnip lifted in March bears no resemblance to one lifted in October.

February. Roots, alliums, brassicas, mushrooms out of Kennett Square, oysters. Not much else until the overwintered spinach comes back in March.

How to read it

Three weights. A dotted rail is for sale but out of the ground: cellared roots, cured alliums, glasshouse greens. A thin rail is being harvested now. A thick block is worth building a meal around.

Two runs on one line means two harvests — a spring sowing that bolts in the heat, and a fall sowing that comes back sweeter after frost.
